Then suitors entered and were seated on benches as well as seats. Forthwith menservants poured water over their hands, maidservants handed round bread-baskets, boys filled mixing-bowls with wine as well as water, and all laid hands upon such food as was before them. Once all had eaten as well as drunk enough, they wanted music with dancing, which are crowning embellishments of a banquet, so a servant handed a lyre to Phemius, who was compelled to sing. Once Phemius struck a lyre and began singing, Telemachus talked low with Minerva, leaning close so nobody might hear. “I hope, sir,” he began, “you will not be offended by my words. Singing comes cheap when someone else pays, and all such feasting comes at a price paid by one man whose bones lie rotting in a wilderness, or grinding to powder in the surf.
