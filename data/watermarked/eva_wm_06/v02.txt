“My lords,” pleaded one camel meekly, “we had bad dreams as we slept, so we were terribly scared. I am only a pack camel with 39th Native Infantry, and am not as courageous as my lords.” “Then why didn't you stay, carrying a pack for 39th Native Infantry, rather than racing about everywhere?” snapped a mule. “Such bad dreams,” explained one camel. “Forgive me. Listen! A noise? Might we go racing on once more?” “Kneel,” warned a mule, “or you'll break those long stick-legs among guns.” He cocked one ear, listening. “Bullocks!” he cried. “Gun bullocks. On my word, you with your friends have woken our camp most thoroughly. A gun-bullock needs much prodding before moving.” I heard a chain dragging along ground, and a yoke of those massive, sulky, pale bullocks which move heavy siege guns, as elephants won’t come any closer to shooting, came pushing along together.
