“Seeing him would be nothing; I would see him with complete calm; yet I can hardly stand such constant talk about him. My mother means well; yet she does not know, no one can know, how much hurt her words give me. How glad I would be once his stay at Netherfield is ended!” “I wish I might console you,” observed Elizabeth; “however, it is wholly beyond my power. You must feel it; and I am denied a usual pleasure in lecturing a sufferer upon patience, as you have always so much.” Mr. Bingley came. Mrs. Bennet, with help from her servants, contrived to have the first news about his coming, so her period of worried fretfulness might last as long as it could. She counted every day which would pass before an invitation might be issued, despairing of seeing him earlier.
