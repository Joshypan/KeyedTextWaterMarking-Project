Greece appears sufficient as an example. Achaeans as well as Ætolians were kept friendly by them, the kingdom of Macedonia was humbled, Antiochus was driven out; yet however much Achaeans and Ætolians achieved, this never secured them leave to increase their power, nor would Philip, however he might plead, ever induce Romans to be friends with him before first humbling him, nor would Antiochus, with all his influence, make them agree to allow him any lordship in that land. As Romans did in such cases what every wise prince ought to do, who has to consider not only present problems, but also future problems, which require preparation with every energy, as, once foreseen, problems are easily remedied; yet, by waiting until problems approach, medicine comes too late, as a disease by then is incurable; for it happens in this as physicians say it happens in hectic fever, that in the beginning of a disease it is easily cured yet difficult to detect; however, in the course of time, having been neither detected nor treated in the beginning, it is easily detected yet difficult to cure.
