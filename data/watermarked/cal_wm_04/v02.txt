Greece appears sufficient as an example. Achaeans as well as Ætolians were made friendly by them, Macedonia was humbled, Antiochus was driven out; yet however much Achaeans with Ætolians had earned, it secured them no leave to increase in power, while Philip, however he might plead, would not induce Romans to be friends with him before first humbling him, nor would Antiochus, with all his influence, make them agree to allow him lordship in any part of that land. As Romans did in such cases what every wise prince ought to do, who has to consider future problems as well as those at hand, which require preparation with every energy, as, once foreseen, problems are easily remedied; yet by waiting until problems approach, medicine comes too late, as a disease by then is incurable; as it happens in this, as physicians say happens in hectic fever, which in its beginning is easily cured yet difficult to detect, while in time, being untreated and undetected in its beginning, it grows easily detected yet difficult to cure.
