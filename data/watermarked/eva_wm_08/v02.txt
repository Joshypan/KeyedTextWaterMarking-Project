“Well, it is most clever,” I admitted, laughing; “yet since, as you just told me, nobody has committed a crime, and nobody was harmed, besides a goose going missing, all such effort appears rather a waste of energy.” Sherlock Holmes was about to answer when our door flew open, and Peterson, our commissionaire, hurried into the room with flushed cheeks, as one dazed with astonishment. “Eh? Well then? Has it returned to life and flown away through a kitchen window?” Holmes twisted himself round upon a couch so as to get a fairer look at Peterson's eager face. “See here, sir! See what my wife found in its crop!” Holding out his hand, he displayed upon its centre a brilliantly sparkling blue gem, rather smaller than a bean, yet so pure, with such radiance, as to twinkle like an electric point in a dark hollow of his hand. Sherlock Holmes sat up, whistling. “By Jove, Peterson!” he cried, “here is treasure trove! I imagine you know what you've got?”
